## What the study involves

You will be asked to put nine everyday objects (e.g. keys, a pen) in places around a room while wearing a phone
on a chest strap that records video. After a break of about 30 minutes you will be asked to find the objects
again: three on your own, three with the help of one phone assistant and three with another. We will time how
long each search takes and ask you to fill in short questionnaires about effort, ease of use and trust. The
session takes about 90 minutes. [Compensation: … / none.]

## What is recorded

- **Video** from the chest camera during the ~15-minute tidy-up phase only. It shows the room, your hands and
  the objects; it is not meant to show your face. It stays on the study phone, which is offline (airplane mode)
  for the whole session, is used only by the assistants during your session, and is **deleted in front of you at
  the end of the session**.
- **Measurements**: search times, whether each object was found, the assistants' answers, and your
  questionnaire answers. These are stored under a code (e.g. "P07"), not your name.
- **About you**: an age band (e.g. 25–34) and how familiar you are with smartphone apps (1–5). Nothing else.
- Not recorded: your name or contact details in the data files, audio, photographs of you, free-text comments.

## Risks and benefits

Risks are minimal: mild physical activity (walking, bending to reach a drawer). Tell us if any hiding place is
uncomfortable to reach and we will skip it. Wearing a camera may feel unusual. There is no direct benefit to you.

## Your rights

- Taking part is voluntary. You may stop at any time during the session without giving a reason; your video is
  then deleted immediately and your measurements are not used.
- After the session you can withdraw your coded data until **[date — e.g. 4 weeks after your session]**, when
  the link between your name and your code is destroyed and the data become anonymous. To withdraw, email
  [researcher email] with your name; we delete all rows with your code.
- Only aggregate results (averages, statistical tests) are published, e.g. in a research paper or a public code
  repository. No video, image or individual record is ever published.
